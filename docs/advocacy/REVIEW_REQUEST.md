## Questions

**Overall**
1. Would you give this URL to a client today? If not, what would have to change first?
2. Is anything on the site unsafe for someone whose phone or computer is watched? (Things we know: the browser history shows the visit; Quick Exit can't erase it; the colorful design is memorable. A plainer theme is planned.)
3. Is anything inaccurate, outdated, or likely to be misread by someone in crisis?

**Copy**
4. Is the language right for someone who hasn't named what's happening to them? Too clinical, too emotional, too long?
5. Is it written for every gender without making anyone feel it isn't for them? Where does it slip?
6. The site is blunt about its limits ("not an emergency service", "alerts can fail", "we can't recover your notes"). Is that the right tone, or does it scare people off tools that would help them?

**Page for men** (for the reviewer experienced with male survivors)
7. Does /for-men.html reflect what men you've worked with actually face: shame and not being believed, fear of being arrested as the aggressor, custody threats, few shelters that accept men? What's missing or wrong?
8. Is the advice about dealing with responding officers safe and realistic?
9. The plan is to say "whatever your gender" once on the home and About pages and then write neutrally, plus this one page, rather than a separate site for men. Does that work?

**Notes**
10. Notes are locked with a PIN that can't be recovered by anyone, including the site. Is that the right trade-off for your clients, and is the warning clear enough?
11. Would notes kept this way be useful for a protective order or custody case? (Export to PDF is planned but not built.) What should an export include?

**Check-ins**
12. Could check-ins put anyone at risk? For example, an abusive partner who pressures someone to set one up, or a contact who is close to the abuser. What should the page warn about?
13. Is /if-you-get-an-alert.html what you'd want a friend or relative to read in that moment?

**Things we've deliberately left out** (your view would change our mind)
14. There's no two-step login, because codes by text or email, or an authenticator app, can be seen on a shared phone. Is that right?
15. An installable app with Emergency and Resources saved for offline use is being added, off unless someone turns it on, named "Solus Vires" with a plain icon. Helpful, or one more thing someone could find on the phone?
