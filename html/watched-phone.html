Your device

If someone has access to your phone or computer

Many people who are being hurt by a partner share a phone plan, a laptop, a password, or a home with the person hurting them. That changes what is safe to do online. This page says plainly what this site protects, what it cannot, and how to use it in a way that leaves less behind.

If you think the device you're holding is being watched right now, the safest step is to use a different one: a friend's phone, a library computer, a work computer if that's safe. Nothing on this site needs your own device. If you're in danger now, call 911, or text START to 88788.

Straight answers

What this site protects, and what it doesn't

Protected: what you write

Your notes, photos and safety plan are encrypted on your device with a PIN before they are saved. The server holds scrambled data it cannot read, and so does anyone who takes a copy of it. Without your PIN, the notes are unreadable, including to us.

Not protected: that you came here

The browser on your device keeps a history of pages you visit, and your phone or internet plan can show which sites were reached. Encryption does not hide the fact that you visited this site. The Quick Exit button leaves the page fast, but it does not erase history.

Protected: your account from guessing

Someone who knows your username cannot lock you out by guessing wrong passwords, and cannot see where you are signed in from. Nobody receives an email when you sign in, because on a shared inbox that email could be the thing that gives you away.

Not protected: a device that is already open

If someone can unlock your phone, they can open anything that is already signed in. The notes lock after a few minutes and when you switch apps, and they need the PIN again. Your account itself stays signed in until you sign out. Sign out when you're done on a device you don't fully control.

Not protected: spyware on the device

Some monitoring apps record the screen or the keys you press. Against that, no website can protect you, including this one. If you suspect it, use a different device for anything that matters and read the tech-safety guidance on the Resources page.

Protected: your contacts' privacy

Your trusted contacts are never shown to each other, and alerts never include where you are. The person who hurts you cannot find your contacts by looking at an alert, because alerts only go to the contacts themselves.

Safer ways to use it

Six habits that leave less behind

1. Use the plain view

The plain version of the site has a neutral look and neutral tab titles, uses no scripts, and reads as an ordinary information page to someone glancing at the screen. Private pages already use neutral titles like "Notes" and "Reminders".

2. Use a private window, then close it

A private or incognito window does not save history, cookies or sign-ins once it is closed. Open one, do what you need, close every private tab. It does not hide your visit from your internet provider or a monitoring app.

3. Clear this site's data when you leave

Browsers let you delete data for one site. In most phone browsers it is under Settings, then Site settings or Website data; look for solusvires.com and remove it. That signs you out and removes any saved offline copy. Clearing all history can itself look suspicious; one site is quieter.

4. Don't install the app on a shared phone

"Add to home screen" puts an icon where anyone using the phone can see it. On a phone you don't fully control, use the site in a browser tab instead, and skip the "Save on this device" offline copy, which stores the Emergency and Resources pages in the browser.

5. Keep check-ins on a device that is yours

The check-in only needs a tap. If your main phone is watched, do check-ins from a device the other person doesn't see, or ask a trusted contact to hold the schedule with you. Changing a check-in schedule on a watched phone is something they could notice.

6. Check where you're signed in

On the Account page, "Where you're signed in" lists every device that has your account open, by browser and device type. If one isn't yours, sign out everywhere else and then change your password. If your notes PIN may be known, change it there too.

When not to use this site

Sometimes the safest thing is to not leave a trace at all

If the person hurting you checks your phone closely, knows your passwords, or has threatened you over things they found on it, consider not using your own device for any of this. A hotline advocate can talk through safer options with you by phone or text, and the Emergency page works from any device with no account.
