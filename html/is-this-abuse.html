Not sure what's happening?

Is this abuse?

If you're asking the question, something probably feels wrong, and that feeling is worth taking seriously. Abuse is a pattern of behavior someone uses to gain power and control over a partner or family member. It isn't always physical, and it can happen to anyone: any gender, any sexuality, any age, in any kind of relationship.

Want to talk it through with someone? The National Domestic Violence Hotline is free, confidential, and open 24/7. You don't need to be sure it's abuse to call. Call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. In danger right now? Call 911.

What it can look like

Abuse takes many forms

Most people experience more than one of these at once. Any one of them, happening again and again, is a warning sign.

Emotional

  • Constant criticism, name-calling, or humiliation
  • Cutting you off from friends and family
  • Extreme jealousy, or accusing you of cheating
  • Making you doubt your own memory ("that never happened")
  • Blaming you for the way they treat you

Physical

  • Hitting, pushing, choking, or grabbing
  • Blocking a doorway or trapping you in a room or home
  • Keeping you from food, sleep, or medical care
  • Hurting or threatening pets or children

Money

  • Controlling all the money or taking your pay
  • Stopping you from working, or getting you fired
  • Running up debt in your name or wrecking your credit

Phones and technology

  • Checking your phone, messages, or location
  • Tracking you with apps, GPS, or shared accounts
  • Harassing you by text or on social media
  • Pressuring you for intimate photos, or threatening to share them

Sexual

  • Any sexual act you didn't agree to, including with a partner or spouse
  • Pressuring you after you've said no, or using guilt
  • Tampering with birth control, or pressuring you about pregnancy

Threats and fear

  • Following you, turning up uninvited, or watching you
  • Threatening to hurt you, themselves, or people you love
  • Threatening to take the kids, call immigration, or "out" you
  • Using religion or culture to justify control

It happens to everyone

Men, and people in same-sex relationships, are hurt too

Abuse isn't defined by who is bigger or stronger. Women abuse men, men abuse men, women abuse women, and it happens across every kind of relationship. People in these situations often hear some version of the same tactics:

  • Being mocked for "letting" it happen, or told no one will believe them
  • Threats to call the police and claim they are the abuser
  • Threats to keep them away from their children
  • Threats to tell family, work, or friends about their sexuality or gender identity

If that sounds familiar, it counts. There is help built for this, and none of it depends on your gender.
