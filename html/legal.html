Legal and rights information

This page is educational only. Laws vary by state and situation.

Solus Vires does not provide legal advice. For case-specific guidance, contact a licensed attorney, legal aid organization, or trained advocate using one of the free resources below.

Free legal help

Where to find real legal aid

WomensLaw.org

Plain-language, state-specific legal information on protective orders, custody, and divorce for abuse survivors, plus a free email legal-info hotline.

womenslaw.org

LawHelp.org

Directory connecting you to free and low-cost legal aid organizations in your state, plus self-help legal forms.

lawhelp.org

ABA Commission on Domestic & Sexual Violence

The American Bar Association's resources on pro bono legal help and attorney referral programs for survivors.

americanbar.org

NIWAP Immigration Resources

Legal-technical library on immigration remedies for immigrant survivors (VAWA self-petitions, U-visas). Written mainly for attorneys and advocates — bring it to one, or ask an advocate to help you use it.

niwaplibrary.wcl.american.edu

Topics survivors often ask about

General education, not case-specific advice — the organizations above can help with your specific situation.

Protective orders

Every state has its own process, timeline, and definition of who can file. WomensLaw's state-by-state pages walk through local filing steps, court expectations, and what documentation may help.

Look up your state

Housing and employment

Many states have laws letting survivors break a lease early or protecting them from workplace discrimination tied to abuse. LawHelp.org can connect you to a local aid organization that knows your state's specific protections.

Find local legal aid

Custody and family court

Family court safety concerns — including how to request safety accommodations during proceedings — should be discussed with a qualified local advocate or attorney whenever possible.
