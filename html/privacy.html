Privacy statement

What we keep, and what we can't see

Written to be read, not skimmed past. Last updated October 2, 2026. Solus Vires is run by Steven Royal through Omnia Royal LLC.

The short version. Reading this site needs no account and leaves nothing with us. If you make an account, your notes are encrypted in your browser with a PIN we never receive, so we can't read them and can't give them to anyone. We use no ads, analytics, or trackers, and we don't log which pages people visit.

If you just read the site

Browsing

  • No account, no cookies from us, no analytics, no advertising, no third-party scripts or fonts.
  • Our web server does not keep access logs, so we don't record your IP address or the pages you open. Error logs are kept only to fix problems, and are overwritten as they fill up.
  • To stop abuse like password guessing, the server briefly holds IP addresses in memory, with a count of requests it refused. They're never written to disk or put in any email, and are gone whenever the server restarts. If one address is refused many times in an hour, we get an email saying so, without the address.
  • The Recovery page can remember which exercises you've done, only on your own device, and you can clear it there. The "Is this abuse?" check stores nothing at all.
  • If you switch on "Plain view", that choice is remembered only on your own device and is never sent to us. Someone who looks through your browser's stored site data could see that it was switched on.
  • If you write to us through the contact form, your message is emailed to our inbox through Brevo. It isn't stored on the site or written to its logs; it's kept in that inbox.
  • If you press "Save on this device" on the Emergency or Resources page, a copy of those pages is kept in your browser so they open without internet. Nothing is saved unless you press it, and the same button removes it. Someone who looks through your browser's stored site data could see it.

If you make an account

What's stored

We store

  • Your username (we never ask for your name, email, or phone number)
  • Your password, only as a one-way hash, and your recovery codes as one-way hashes that also need a secret key kept outside the database
  • Your notes, private profile, and safety plan, only as encrypted data we can't read
  • Photos or screenshots you attach to a note, only as encrypted data we can't read. Before a photo is locked, your browser removes the hidden details saved inside it, such as where it was taken. The date it was taken is kept, inside the encrypted part
  • When your account was created and when you last logged in
  • A login cookie while you're signed in (it expires after 14 days)

If you use check-ins, we also store

  • Each trusted contact's nickname and email address, and whether they accepted
  • Your schedule and when you last checked in
  • For contacts who turn on notifications, a technical address for their device (no name or location), when it last accepted an alert, and when it stopped working
  • A history of alerts sent for you: when, which kind (an alert or an "all clear"), and how many contacts, emails, and notifications. Counts only, not who or what was said. It's kept for 90 days, you can clear it at any time, and it's deleted with your account
Your notes PIN never leaves your browser. Your notes are locked with it before they're sent, so the database holds only scrambled data. That's also why we can't recover your notes if you forget your PIN.

If someone demands your data

What a court order or a breach could and couldn't reveal

Could reveal

  • That an account with your username exists, and when it was created and last used
  • Your trusted contacts' nicknames and email addresses
  • Your check-in schedule and times, and the last 90 days of alert history (unless you cleared it)
  • How many notes and photos you have, how large each photo is, and when they were saved (not what they say or show)
  • Whether you've saved a safety plan, and when it was last changed (not what's in it)
  • Messages sent through the contact form, which are in our email inbox, not on the site

Could not reveal

  • What your notes, profile, or safety plan say, or what your photos show
  • Your PIN or your password
  • Your real name, phone number, or location (we never collect them)
  • Which pages you visited (we don't log it)

Other companies involved

Who else handles data

Cloudflare

Protects the site and delivers it to you, so it handles every visit, including your IP address, under its own privacy policy. It may set a strictly necessary security cookie.

DigitalOcean

Hosts our server and database in the United States.

Brevo

Sends check-in invitations, alerts, and "all clear" messages by email. It receives the contact's email address and your username, which appears in the message. It also carries contact-form messages to our inbox, including whatever you write in them.

Browser push services

If a trusted contact turns on notifications, alerts travel through their browser's push service (such as Google, Apple, or Mozilla), encrypted in transit.

Your control

Deleting your data, and how long we keep it

You can delete your account at any time from the Notes & Check-ins page. That permanently removes your notes, profile, contacts, schedule, alert history, and logins from our database at once. Encrypted nightly backups, including one encrypted copy stored with our hosting provider, are kept for up to 30 days, so deleted data disappears from those within 30 days. The backups can only be opened with a key that is not kept on any server. Trusted contacts can stop alerts at any time from the link in any email. We don't yet delete inactive accounts automatically; that policy is being written.

Terms, in plain words

What you can and can't rely on this site for

By using the site you're agreeing to these. They're short on purpose.

It isn't an emergency service

Nobody watches this site around the clock, and it can't send help. Check-in alerts are sent automatically by our server, so they depend on the server, email, and push notifications all working, and on your contacts seeing the alert and acting on it. An alert can be late or never arrive. If you're in danger, call 911.

It isn't legal or professional advice

What's here is general information, not advice about your situation, and not a substitute for an advocate, lawyer, or counselor. Laws are different in every state. Every phone number and organization was checked against its own website before it was added, but they can change; the organization's own site is the final word.

It's still a prototype

Accounts are invite-only. The site hasn't yet been reviewed by legal counsel or a domestic violence advocacy organization, and its security hasn't been independently audited. Features may change, and we may have to pause accounts or check-ins. You can print or save a copy of your notes from the Notes page. That copy is not encrypted: anyone who finds the file or the printout can read it, so keep it somewhere the person you're worried about can't reach.

Use it for yourself, not on someone else

Notes and check-ins are for your own safety. Don't use them to watch, track, or contact someone who hasn't agreed. Trusted contacts choose whether to accept and can stop at any time. Accounts used to harass someone may be closed. The site is free and offered as it is, without guarantees.

Not yet reviewed

An honest caveat

This statement and these terms describe exactly how the site works today, but they haven't yet been reviewed by a lawyer. They will be before the site opens to the public, and this page will say when they change.

If you are in immediate danger, call 911 (or your local emergency number).
