What a Solus Vires export is, and what it proves

Someone may hand you a printed or PDF export of their private notes from this site, perhaps with a verification file. This page explains what you are holding, in the plainest terms we can manage, so you can decide how much weight to give it. It was written by the site's operator and has not yet been reviewed by counsel; that review is being sought and this page will say when it is done.

In one sentence: the export is the survivor's own contemporaneous record, kept encrypted so that nobody but them could read it, together with the server's signed timestamps showing that each entry existed in its current form at a stated time and has not been changed since. It is tamper-evidence. It is not a certification by us of what happened.

The log

How the notes are kept

Written by the survivor, on their device

Each entry has a date the writer chose and the time it was first saved. Entries can be edited or deleted by the writer. Photos can be attached; before a photo is saved, the browser redraws it to strip hidden metadata such as location, so the image is kept but its embedded data is not.

Encrypted before it leaves the device

Notes, photos, the private profile and the safety plan are encrypted in the browser with a key derived from a PIN the server never receives (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds; AES-256-GCM). The server stores only the encrypted form. We cannot read it, recover it, or reset the PIN.

Decrypted only to make the export

The export is produced on the survivor's device after they enter their PIN. The readable copy, printed or saved, exists only where they put it. Nothing readable is sent to us to make it.

What the server records about each save

Each time an item is saved, the server records the SHA-256 fingerprint of the encrypted data it received, the time, and an Ed25519 signature over both. It also records the reason for a later version: edited by the writer, or re-encrypted when they changed their PIN. These rows hold fingerprints, never content.

The signed export

What verification shows, and what it cannot

It shows

  • That the server held each entry, in exactly its current encrypted form, at the signed time.
  • That the entry has not been altered on the server since, and that any earlier versions are listed with their times and reasons.
  • With the writer's PIN, that the readable text in the export is what the signed encrypted data decrypts to.

It cannot show

  • That the events described happened, or happened on the date the writer gave. The signed time is when the entry was saved; the date in the entry is the writer's.
  • Who typed the entry. Accounts are a username and password; we hold no identity documents.
  • Anything about entries the writer deleted. Deleted entries leave no trace, by design.

Verifying it yourself

Ask for the verification file (a .json file made at the same time as the export). Open solusvires.com/verify.html, load the file, and compare the key id it reports with the one published below. With the survivor's PIN entered on that page, the text is compared too. The page works offline once loaded and sends nothing.

Requests to us

What a subpoena or request can and cannot obtain

From our privacy statement, which is written from the code. We will respond to valid legal process, and this is what exists to produce.

Exists, and could be produced

  • That an account with a given username exists, when it was created and last used.
  • The encrypted notes, photos, profile and safety plan, as unreadable data, and the signed fingerprints and times described above.
  • How many notes and photos there are and when they were saved; whether a safety plan exists and when it changed.
  • Trusted contacts' nicknames and email addresses, the check-in schedule, the message to contacts if one was written, and the last 90 days of alert history.

Does not exist, so cannot be produced

  • The readable content of any note, photo, profile or safety plan. We hold no key.
  • The PIN or the password.
  • Real names, phone numbers, or locations. We never collect them.
  • Which pages anyone visited, or from where. The servers keep no access logs and no IP history.
