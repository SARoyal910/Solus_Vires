In the next ten minutes

  1. Reach out the way you normally would. A text or a call that sounds like you. "Hey, thinking of you, call me when you can." Don't mention the alert unless you know their phone is safe.
  2. Give it a little time. Most missed check-ins are a flat battery, a long shift or a forgotten tap. The alert repeats until they check in, so you'll know if it continues.
  3. Don't contact the person they may be afraid of, and don't post anything or ask around in a way that could get back to that person.
  4. If you can't reach them and you believe they're in danger, call 911 or the local emergency number and ask for a welfare check. Say where they live and that you're worried about domestic violence. You don't need proof.
  5. If you want to talk it through first, the National Domestic Violence Hotline talks with worried friends and family every day: 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.
