For friends, family, and coworkers

How to help someone you care about

People being abused are often cut off from the people around them, so a steady, trusted person makes a real difference. You don't need to fix it or have the right words. The most useful thing you can ask is simple: "How can I help?"

You can call too. Hotline advocates talk with worried friends and family every day, 24/7: 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

What helps

Do

Believe them

Listen without judging or questioning their story. Being believed is often the first step toward getting help.

Let them lead

They know their situation, and their safety, better than anyone. Respect their choices and their timing, even if you'd choose differently.

Stay in touch

Keep calling, texting, and inviting them, even if they cancel. Abuse isolates people; your steady contact counters that.

Share resources quietly

Offer the Hotline number or this site in a way that's safe to keep, like a saved contact under a different name. Offer to be there while they call.

Help them plan

Planning for safety works best with an advocate. You can offer practical help: a place to keep copies of documents, a spare key, a code word that means "call for help."

Write things down

If you see injuries, threats, or damage, note the date, time, and what you saw. It can help later with a protective order, a custody case, or charges.

What to avoid

Don't

  • Don't confront the person hurting them. It can make things more dangerous for them, and for you.
  • Don't push them to leave right now. Leaving is a personal decision, and it's often the most dangerous time. Pressure can push them away from you.
  • Don't blame or judge. Avoid "why do you stay?" or "why didn't you fight back?"
  • Don't make decisions for them, including calling the police when they aren't in immediate danger. Ask first.
  • Don't post about it or discuss it where the abuser, or people close to them, could see.
  • Don't give up on them if they stay or go back. Stay someone they can come to.

If the person is a man

Men need to be believed too

Men are often mocked or doubted when they say a partner is hurting them, so many never tell anyone. Take him seriously, don't joke about it, and don't ask why he didn't just leave or fight back.

When to call the police

Call 911 if someone is in immediate danger

If you believe someone is being hurt right now, or their life is at risk, call 911. Otherwise, talk with them first about whether they want police involved: calling without their agreement can put them at more risk once officers leave. If you hear abuse happening next door, a safe interruption, like knocking to ask a question, can also help.

Look after yourself too

Warning signs to take seriously

Danger often rises when someone is trying to leave. If they talk about ending their life, give things away, or suddenly withdraw, or if the abuser has access to a gun, get help right away: call or text 988 for a crisis counselor, or 911 if someone's life is in danger. And if the abuser threatens you, take it seriously and write it down.

If you are in immediate danger, call 911 (or your local emergency number).
