For men

It happens to men too, and it counts

If a wife, girlfriend, husband, or boyfriend hurts you, controls you, or makes you afraid, that is abuse. It is not weakness, and it is not something you should just take. About 1 in 10 men in the US has experienced rape, physical violence, or stalking by a partner, and about 1 in 7 has experienced severe physical violence from one.

Help is for you too. The National Domestic Violence Hotline serves men, free, confidential, and 24/7. Call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. In danger right now? Call 911.

Why it's hard to say out loud

You're not the only one who has stayed quiet

Many men never tell anyone. These are some of the reasons, and none of them mean it isn't real.

"Who would believe me?"

Men are often told abuse is something that happens to other people, or it's treated as a joke. Being disbelieved is one of the most common fears men describe. Advocates on the Hotline hear from men every day and will take you seriously.

"I should be able to handle it"

A lot of abuse against men targets their sense of being a man: being called weak, not a real man, not earning enough, or mocked for being upset. That's a tactic, not a truth.

"I'm bigger, so it can't be abuse"

Abuse is about power and control, not size. Constant put-downs, controlling money, cutting you off from people, and threats are abuse whether or not anyone throws a punch.

If you're afraid of being blamed

"They'll say I'm the abuser"

Threatening to call the police and claim you did it is a common way to keep someone quiet. Things that help:

Keep a dated record

Write down what happens and when, and save texts, voicemails, and photos of injuries somewhere your partner can't get to. Records made at the time are hard to argue with.

Get legal advice early

If you've been threatened with a false report, talk to a lawyer about it. Making that threat may itself be a crime where you live. Protective orders are available to men as well as women.

WomensLaw on false-report threats

Your children

If you're worried about losing access to your kids, a family-law attorney or legal aid office can explain your options in your state. Keep records of your involvement in their care.

WomensLaw.org is named for women, but its legal information applies to victims of every gender. It has a section written for male victims: womenslaw.org male victims. Nothing on this site is legal advice.

Somewhere to go

Shelter and safe housing

Many people assume shelters are only for women. Programs that receive federal funding are required to serve victims of every gender. If a local program can't take you in, ask what else it can do. A Hotline advocate can help you find options, and help if you've been turned away because you're a man.
